The Nike Air Force 1 is about as simple as a sneaker gets, and that is part of why people keep buying it. It is also one of the most copied sneakers around, so a little know-how goes a long way. Here is how we think about sizing, break-in and checking a pair before you buy.

## A quick bit of background

Designer Bruce Kilgore's Air Force 1 came out in 1982 and was the first basketball shoe to use Nike Air cushioning. It is a leather cupsole shoe, so the upper sits down inside a thick rubber cup. That is why it feels solid and a little stiff out of the box.

## Do Air Force 1s run big?

For a lot of people, yes, a bit. The most common advice you will hear is to go true to size or half a size down from your usual Nike size. The reason is the roomy toe box. Plenty of folks find their normal size leaves some extra space up front, especially with the low-top version.

Here is the general rule of thumb:

- **Normal to narrow feet:** try half a size down from your usual size if you like a snug fit. Stay true to size if you like room for thick socks.
- **Wide feet:** stay true to size. Sizing down can pinch across the ball of the foot.
- **Between sizes:** the leather stretches and softens a little over time, so many people pick the smaller size of the two.

Fit still comes down to your own foot, so treat that as a starting point. Youth and grade school sizes use their own scale, so check the size label on the listing. ## Break-in and care

New Air Force 1s are stiff across the toe box and tend to crease right where your foot bends. That is normal for leather. A few days of wear loosens them up. Some people use shoe trees or a crease guard, and others call it character.

For care, a damp cloth and a soft brush handle most scuffs on a leather upper. If you live somewhere hot and humid like Southwest Florida, keep your pairs out of a hot car and out of damp closets. Heat and moisture are tough on glue and on foam over the years.

## What do '07 and LV8 mean?

You will see these on listings and on our model pages:

- **'07:** this is the standard version of the shoe, built to look like the original. When someone just says Air Force 1, this is usually what they mean. See our [Air Force 1 '07 page](/nike-air-force-1-07/).
- **LV8:** short for "Level 8." Nike uses it for versions with special materials, colors or extra details. It is still an Air Force 1 underneath, so the sizing tips above apply. See our [Air Force 1 '07 LV8 page](/nike-air-force-1-07-lv8/).

## How to spot a real pair

Be wary of any claim that one single trick proves a pair is real, because copies keep getting better. Instead, stack up several small checks. Here are the basics:

1. **Match the style code.** Every Nike pair has a style code, a mix of letters and numbers like CW2288-111. It should match on the box label, the tag inside the shoe, and the listing. On our model pages the style code sits right under the name.
2. **Look at the box.** The label should be clean and clear, with the model name, size and color matching the shoe. Messy printing or a mismatched label is a red flag.
3. **Check the overall shape.** A real pair has a clean, even sole, even stitching and a rounded, well-shaped toe box. A toe box that looks flat, boxy or lopsided deserves a closer look.
4. **Check the materials.** The leather should feel and look consistent, and the shoe should not give off a strong chemical glue smell.
5. **Read the whole listing.** Look for clear photos of the actual pair, the size tag and the box, not just stock images. Check the return policy too.
6. **Trust the price.** If a brand new pair is priced far below everywhere else, ask yourself why.
