The Air Jordan 1 comes in three heights, and shoppers ask us all the time which one to get. The short answer is that it depends on how you wear them. Here is a straight comparison so you can pick without guessing.

## Where the Air Jordan 1 came from

The Air Jordan 1 debuted in 1985 as Michael Jordan's first signature shoe with Nike, and the original was a high-top. That original shape is the one collectors usually call the real Jordan 1. The Mid and Low versions came after, built around the same design but cut lower on the ankle.

## The three heights at a glance

- **High:** the tallest collar and the classic look. This is the version people usually picture when they say Jordan 1. It covers the ankle and has the most leather.
- **Mid:** a lower collar than the High that still covers the ankle. It keeps the high-top feel without as much height.
- **Low:** sits below the ankle like a regular low sneaker. It is the easiest to throw on and the one most people wear in warm weather.

## Look and style

The High is the statement. It looks best with pants that stack or sit over the top of the shoe, and it is the one most people think of as the original. The Low works well with shorts, cuffed jeans and chinos, and it is the lightest looking of the three. The Mid sits in the middle and works with just about everything, which is why plenty of people call it the easy all-rounder.

All three come in loads of colorways, and the Special Edition versions (marked SE in the name) bring different materials or color blocking. ## Fit and sizing

Most people find Air Jordan 1s fit true to size, with a fairly narrow, structured toe area. If you have wider feet, think about going up half a size, since the leather does not stretch much across the front. If you like a snug fit, stay true to size.

The ankle height changes how the shoe feels more than the size does. High and Mid collars wrap your ankle and need a little break-in at the top edge. The Low has no collar to rub, so it feels easier from the first wear. As always, fit varies by foot, so use this as a starting point and check the US size on each listing.

## Comfort and day-to-day wear

The Jordan 1 is a classic from the 1980s, so the cushioning is simple compared with a modern running shoe. Expect a firm, flat feel. If you plan to be on your feet all day, think about an insole.

Some versions have updated comfort tech. The Air Jordan 1 High Zoom Comfort, for example, adds Zoom Air cushioning for a softer feel. You can find it on our [Air Jordan 1 High Zoom Comfort page](/jordan-1-high-zoom-comfort/).

## Heat, weather and upkeep

If you live in Southwest Florida, you already know it is hot most of the year and rain can show up out of nowhere. A Low breathes better and dries quicker than a High. That is one reason low-tops are so popular down here. A High has more leather around the ankle, which feels warmer in the heat.

Leather uppers clean up well with a damp cloth and a soft brush. Keep light-colored pairs away from wet grass and dirt as much as you can. Avoid leaving any pair in a hot car, since heat is tough on glue.

## Which one should you buy?

- **Pick the High** if you want the original look, you like the ankle support feel, and you wear sneakers with longer pants.
- **Pick the Mid** if you want a high-top style without the full height, and a shoe that goes with most outfits.
- **Pick the Low** if you wear sneakers in warm weather, wear shorts a lot, or want the easiest everyday pair.

If you are not sure, many people start with a Low or a Mid because they are easy to wear and to style, then add a High later.
