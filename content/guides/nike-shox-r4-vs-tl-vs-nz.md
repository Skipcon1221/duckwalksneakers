Nike Shox are the sneakers with the visible foam columns under the heel. They had a big moment in the early 2000s, and they came back in a big way. The tricky part is that Shox is a whole family of shoes, and the R4, TL and NZ look and wear quite differently. Here is a simple way to tell them apart.

## What Shox cushioning is

Shox uses a set of foam columns held between two plates in the sole. The idea is that the columns compress when your foot lands and spring back as you lift off. Where the columns sit and how many there are is the main way to tell the models apart.

## Nike Shox R4

The Shox R4 came out in 2000 and was the first Shox shoe. The "R" stands for running, and the 4 is for the four columns under the heel. The columns sit only at the back of the shoe, so the rest of the sole stays low and flat.

- **Look:** a sleek, runner-style profile with the four columns showing at the heel.
- **Upper:** typically a mix of mesh and synthetic overlays.
- **Best for:** people who want the Shox look in the most understated, runner-style shape.

## Nike Shox TL

The Shox TL arrived in 2003, and TL stands for "total." That is because the columns run the whole length of the sole, with 12 of them in a row along the bottom. It is the bulkiest and most eye-catching of the three.

- **Look:** tall, chunky sole with columns along the whole shoe, and a bold upper with plenty of overlays.
- **Upper:** panels of mesh, synthetic leather and plastic detailing.
- **Best for:** people who want the full Shox statement and do not mind a thick platform.

## Nike Shox NZ

The Shox NZ showed up in the early 2000s too. It uses a lower, slimmer Shox setup than the TL and is usually made with leather or leather-look materials instead of a technical mesh. It looks the least like a running shoe of the three.

- **Look:** the quietest of the family, with a lower heel and a cleaner upper.
- **Upper:** often leather or leather-style synthetic.
- **Best for:** people who want the Shox feel but a more everyday look that goes with jeans.

## Fit and sizing

People most often say Shox fit close to true to size, but the chunky builds can feel different from model to model. The TL has a lot of structure and a thick tongue, so it can feel snug if you have wide feet. The R4 has a lower, runner-style upper that most people find less bulky. If you are between sizes, think about your sock thickness and how roomy you like a shoe.

Because fit is personal, use the US size on the listing as the key fact, and keep your return options in mind when you check out on eBay.

## What to check when you buy

- **Match the style code.** Our model pages show the style code right under the name. Check it matches the colorway you want.
- **Look at the columns.** The columns should look clean and even, with no cracks or crumbling. Older vintage pairs from the early 2000s are more likely to have aging foam than newer re-releases, so check the condition line on the listing.
- **Read the condition.** We list each pair with its condition and size, so you know what you are getting.

If you live in Southwest Florida, keep your Shox out of hot garages and parked cars. Heat and humidity are not friendly to foam and glue over time, so store them in a cool, dry spot.

- **R4:** pick it if you want a slimmer shoe with just a hint of the Shox look.
- **TL:** pick it if you want the full Shox look and the biggest, boldest sole.
- **NZ:** pick it if you want something lower and cleaner that still has the Shox cushioning.
